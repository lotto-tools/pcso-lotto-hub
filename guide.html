PCSO claiming guide

How to claim a PCSO lotto prize in 2026

Where to go with a winning ticket, which IDs to bring, exactly what the 20% tax takes out of a jackpot, the one-year deadline, and what the 1% agent commission is — written from PCSO's own claiming rules and the TRAIN Law.

1. Where to claim, by prize amount

A PCSO lotto ticket is a bearer instrument: the person holding the signed original ticket is the one paid. Which office pays depends only on how much the prize is worth.

Prize amountWhere to claimHow it is paidTax
₱20 – ₱10,000 Any authorized PCSO lotto outlet, or a PCSO branch office Cash, subject to the outlet's cash on hand None
₱10,001 – ₱300,000 A PCSO branch office near you, or the PCSO Main Office Cash up to ₱60,000 gross; check above that 20% final tax
Over ₱300,000, including every jackpot PCSO Main Office only — Sun Plaza, Shaw Blvd. cor. Princeton St., Mandaluyong City Check in the winner's name, validated in person 20% final tax

A prize of ₱20 is the smallest amount a ticket pays. If the amount printed on your ticket is below ₱10,000 and the outlet where you bought it has the cash, that is the fastest route: the clerk validates the barcode and pays on the spot. Larger prizes move up the chain because the ticket has to be verified against the draw record.

For a jackpot, call the PCSO Main Office before travelling. Processing a jackpot is not a same-hour transaction, and the office will tell you its current requirements and hours.

2. Claiming step by step

  1. Check the draw yourself. Compare your numbers with the result for that exact draw date and game on our results page, then re-check against PCSO's published result. Only PCSO validation is final.
  2. Sign the back of the ticket and write your name on it. Do this now, whether or not the ticket won — PCSO pays the person whose signature is on the ticket.
  3. Keep the ticket flat, dry and readable. Do not fold the barcode, laminate it with heat, or let it get wet. A ticket the terminal cannot read may be refused.
  4. Choose the right office from the bracket table above.
  5. Bring your documents — the original ticket and two valid government IDs for anything above ₱10,000. Bring a pen; there is a form to fill in.
  6. Validation. Staff scan the ticket and match it to the draw record. You may be asked a few questions about the purchase.
  7. Payment after tax. PCSO deducts the 20% final tax and pays the rest in cash or by check, depending on the amount.
  8. Keep the receipt and any acknowledgement PCSO gives you, and photograph the ticket's front and back before handing it over.

3. What to bring

  • The original winning ticket, signed at the back. Photocopies and photos are not accepted in place of the ticket.
  • Two valid government-issued IDs for prizes above ₱10,000, both with your photo and signature — a passport, driver's licence, UMID, PhilSys national ID, postal ID, PRC licence, SSS or GSIS ID. The signature on your IDs is matched against the one on the ticket.
  • A Winner's Profile Form, available at PCSO branches and the Main Office and filled in on site.
  • A special power of attorney if someone else must claim for you, plus that person's own valid ID. PCSO does not release a prize to a representative without one.

For an outlet-level claim of ₱10,000 or less, the signed ticket and one valid ID are normally enough.

4. The 20% tax on prizes

Under Section 24(B)(1) of the Tax Code as amended by the TRAIN Law (Republic Act No. 10963, in force since 1 January 2018), winnings from PCSO lotto of ₱10,000 or less are exempt, while prizes above ₱10,000 carry a 20% final tax.

The 20% applies to the whole prize, not only to the part above ₱10,000. The difference is a cliff:

Gross prizeTaxYou receive
₱10,000.00₱0.00₱10,000.00
₱10,000.01₱2,000.00₱8,000.01
₱50,000.00₱10,000.00₱40,000.00
₱300,000.00₱60,000.00₱240,000.00
₱537,913,610.81₱107,582,722.16₱430,330,888.65

The tax is withheld by PCSO when the prize is paid. You never pay it in advance, and anyone who asks you to send a “tax deposit” or a “release fee” before a payout is running a scam. Our tax and prize calculator does this arithmetic for any amount, including the latest jackpots.

5. What the 1% agent commission is

When a jackpot ticket is sold, PCSO pays a 1% commission to the authorized lotto agent — the outlet that sold the winning combination. It comes out of PCSO's side of the pool, not out of the winner's prize. A winner is never charged it and it does not change the 20% tax figure.

It is one of the reasons PCSO advises players to buy only from authorized outlets: an unauthorized seller's ticket is not a valid PCSO ticket and cannot be claimed at all.

6. The one-year deadline

A winning ticket is valid for one year from the date of the draw. This is in PCSO's charter, as amended by Batas Pambansa Blg. 42: a prize not claimed within one year is considered forfeited and forms part of the charity fund. The rule applies to every prize, from ₱20 to a nine-figure jackpot, and there is no extension for a ticket found late.

Work out the deadline the day you check your numbers, and go early rather than in the last week — a weekend, a holiday or a missing document at the last minute can cost you the prize.

8. How prize scams work, and how to refuse one

PCSO does not contact winners. There is no text, call, email or social media message telling you that you have won. You find out by checking the numbers yourself and presenting the ticket. Anything that begins with someone telling you you won is fraud.

  • Refuse any request for an up-front “processing fee”, “tax payment” or “release fee”. Tax is deducted from the prize, never collected from you.
  • Never send a photo of your ticket, your bank details or a one-time code to a person or page that claims it will “verify” a win.
  • Do not let a middleman “handle” a claim for a share of the prize. Go to PCSO yourself, or with a lawyer you chose.
  • Buy only from authorized PCSO outlets. No website, including this one, can sell you a PCSO ticket.

10. Frequently asked questions

How much tax will be taken from my prize?

Nothing up to ₱10,000. Above ₱10,000, 20% of the gross prize under the TRAIN Law. A ₱1,000,000 prize pays ₱200,000 in tax and ₱800,000 to you.

Is the 20% taken from the amount above ₱10,000 only?

No. Once the prize exceeds ₱10,000 the 20% is computed on the whole amount. A ₱10,000 prize is paid in full; a ₱10,000.01 prize pays ₱2,000.00 in tax.

Where exactly is the PCSO Main Office?

Sun Plaza, Shaw Boulevard corner Princeton Street, Mandaluyong City. Jackpot claims are processed there in person and paid by check in the winner's name.

How long do I have to claim?

One year from the draw date. Unclaimed prizes are forfeited to PCSO's charity fund, with no extension.

What if my ticket is torn, wet or unreadable?

The barcode has to scan. A mutilated or unreadable ticket may be refused, so keep it flat, dry and out of direct heat from the moment you buy it.

Can someone else claim for me?

Only with a special power of attorney and their own valid ID, presented at the PCSO office. The safest course is to claim in person with the ticket signed in your name.

Is the jackpot paid in installments?

No. PCSO pays a jackpot as a lump sum, by check, after the 20% tax is withheld.

What is the 1% agent commission?

A commission PCSO pays the outlet that sold the winning jackpot ticket. It is not deducted from the winner's prize.

Can I buy a PCSO ticket through this website?

No. We publish results and statistics only. Tickets are sold at authorized PCSO outlets; PCSO's own online channel, where it is available, is the only official way to play without visiting an outlet.

Do hot or overdue numbers improve my chances?

No. Each draw is independent and every combination has the same probability — 1 in 40,475,358 for Ultra Lotto 6/58. Statistics describe past draws, not the next one. Our statistics page is there to explore the data, not to predict it.
